## 1. What the test is

The Inner System Map is a self-report reflection tool built for Transcendent Institute, a school teaching a Self Leadership Program that integrates the psychology of Internal Family Systems (IFS) with Kabbalah, Yoga and Andean tradition. The test maps how a person's inner system is organised right now: how much Self-leadership is available, which group of parts is currently leading (Managers, Firefighters or Exiles), and which specific parts are most active within each group.

It is a screener that generates hypotheses for reflection and for facilitated work. It is not a validated psychometric instrument, not a diagnostic tool, not therapy, not a quick fix and not a religion. These limits are stated on the landing page and on every results page, and all bands and thresholds are the school's heuristics, never norms.

Two modes exist. Public mode is the lead tool on the school's website: anyone over 18 can take it; answers are scored in the browser; the person gives a name and email at the start and receives the results as a PDF, with a copy to the institute. Cohort mode is for program participants: they join with an access code from their facilitator, sign in by an emailed code, consent explicitly to storage and facilitator visibility, and their attempts are kept with history; facilitators see a dashboard.


## 2. Theoretical framing the test must respect

Source theory: Richard C. Schwartz, Internal Family Systems Therapy (Guilford, 1995); Schwartz and Sweezy, Internal Family Systems Therapy, 2nd ed. (Guilford, 2020); Schwartz, No Bad Parts (Sounds True, 2021). Measurement precedent: DeLand, Strongin and Schwartz (2006), the IFS Scale, from which no items were copied.

**The model.** The mind is a system of parts plus a core Self. The Self is undamaged in everyone and is the natural leader; when it does not lead it is constrained by parts, not defective. Parts take on three roles. Exiles are young, vulnerable parts carrying burdens (pain, shame, fear, worthlessness, loneliness), kept out of awareness. Managers protect proactively, organising daily life so exiles are never triggered. Firefighters protect reactively, acting fast once exile pain breaks through, whatever the cost. Manager, Firefighter and Exile are roles a part was forced into, not its nature.

**IFS does not type people.** Nobody is “a Perfectionist”. All copy uses part language: “a part of you that…”, never “you are a…”. Results are a map of a system, never a label.

**No bad parts.** Every protector formed for a reason; every exile carries something that was not its fault. Tone toward every part is respectful and curious. No result is bad.

**Blending is what “leading” means.** A part leads when it blends with the person and acts in place of the Self. The test reports which parts most often take the lead and how much Self-leadership is available alongside them. Self is measured as its own dimension, not as the absence of parts.

**Exiles are hidden by definition.** Self-report can only detect exiles when they break through. A high exile score means exile feelings are flooding often. A low exile score with strong protectors does not mean there are no exiles; it more likely means the protectors are succeeding. The results say this.

**Hypotheses, not verdicts.** Real knowing comes from turning toward a part and asking what it protects and fears. Exiles are approached only with the permission of their protectors. The app never invites the person to go to an exile directly; the one exercise addresses the leading protector only.

**Program context.** In the Self Leadership Program the tool belongs to Level II, Balance, which works with the Sefirah Yesod and the Andean Jaguar of Kay Pacha. The Self corresponds, in the school's synthesis, to Tiferet. This is presented as the school's interpretive synthesis, not as a claim IFS or those traditions make.

Level II text shown to cohort participants: “In the Self Leadership Program this map belongs to Level II, Balance, which works with the Sefirah Yesod (the foundation: self-image, conditioning, the relational template) and with the Jaguar of Kay Pacha in the Andean tradition. The jaguar's fangs, claws and muscles are its survival strategies. Your protectors are your fangs and claws. They were needed. The work is to understand them, not remove them. In this school's synthesis the Self corresponds to Tiferet, the heart and centre of the Tree of Life, and the guiding question of the program is: who is actually leading your inner life right now?”


## 3. Structure of the instrument

| Block | Scales | Items (full) | Items (short) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self | SELF | 8 | 6 |
| Managers | PERF, CRIT, PLEA, CTRL, INTL, AVOI, CARE, HYPV, DIST | 36 | 27 |
| Firefighters | NUMB, DISS, ANGR, IMPL | 16 | 12 |
| Mixed role | REBL | 4 | 3 |
| Exiles (burden themes) | SHAM, ABAN, FEAR, POWL, LONE | 20 | 15 |
| Total | 20 scales | 84 | 63 |

Two forms share one item bank. The full form (84 items) is the default in cohort mode (full); the short form (63 items, marked below) is the default in public mode (short). Scoring works on whichever items were answered.

Response scale, frequency based, five points: 1 Never or almost never, 2 Rarely, 3 Sometimes, 4 Often, 5 Almost always. Instruction shown before starting: “Answer for how you have actually been over the last 6 months, not how you would like to be. There are no right answers. Go with your first response. Some statements touch tender places. You can pause or stop at any time.”

Item order is randomised per attempt with two constraints: two items from the same scale never appear consecutively, and no more than two exile items appear in any run of five. The seed is stored so an order can be reproduced. Scale and block names are never shown during the questionnaire. One item per screen with auto-advance, a progress bar and a back button; progress is saved on the device so a refresh loses nothing. Every item must be answered.

Deliberate exclusion: there is no self-harm or suicidality scale. A public self-report tool with no clinician in the loop must not screen for this.

Scales marked “proposed” were added in version 2 so that firefighters and exiles can be measured as groups; they have no source in the school's original handout and await clinical review: DISS, IMPL, SHAM, ABAN, FEAR, POWL, LONE.


## 4. The item bank (English master)

Asterisk = short-form item. Item IDs are stable and scoring never depends on wording; translations in Spanish, Romanian and Turkish are keyed by ID.


### Block A. Self

**SELF. Self-leadership**
- SELF1* When something upsets me, I can find my way back to calm.
- SELF2* I can be curious about my reactions instead of judging them.
- SELF3* I can feel compassion for the sides of me I do not like.
- SELF4* Even under pressure, I can see clearly what matters.
- SELF5* I can stay with a difficult feeling without being taken over by it.
- SELF6* When two sides of me pull in different directions, I can listen to both.
- SELF7 I trust myself to handle what life brings.
- SELF8 I feel connected to other people and to life.


### Block B. Managers

**PERF. Perfectionist / Demanding**
- PERF1* I re-check or redo work that is already good enough.
- PERF2* I delay sharing or handing in my work because it never feels ready.
- PERF3* I demand more of myself than I would ever demand of anyone else.
- PERF4 I find it hard to enjoy a result because I see what could have been better.

**CRIT. Critic / Judge**
- CRIT1* I criticise myself before anyone else gets the chance.
- CRIT2* When I receive feedback, I am harder on myself than the other person was.
- CRIT3* When I feel exposed or hurt, I get harsh, with myself or with the other person.
- CRIT4 I notice other people's faults quickly and find it hard to let them go.

**PLEA. Pleaser**
- PLEA1* I say yes when I want to say no.
- PLEA2* I soften or change my opinion to avoid disagreement.
- PLEA3* When someone is displeased with me, I cannot rest until it is repaired.
- PLEA4 Around authority figures I become more agreeable than I really am.

**CTRL. Controller / Planner**
- CTRL1* Last-minute changes of plan unsettle me more than they seem to unsettle others.
- CTRL2* I run through possible scenarios in advance because I cannot stand being caught off guard.
- CTRL3* I find it hard to delegate because it will not be done properly.
- CTRL4 I need clear structure and rules before I can relax.

**INTL. Intellectualizer / Rationalizer**
- INTL1* When a conversation turns emotional, I start explaining or analyzing.
- INTL2* I can describe what I feel much better than I can actually feel it.
- INTL3* I need to understand why I feel something before I let myself feel it.
- INTL4 People close to me say I live in my head.

**AVOI. Avoider / Procrastinator**
- AVOI1* I avoid starting the things that matter most to me, because they might not go well.
- AVOI2* When an important decision is waiting for me, I keep myself busy with other things instead.
- AVOI3* I stay away from situations where my performance will be evaluated.
- AVOI4 I only start when the deadline leaves me no choice.

**CARE. Caretaker / Rescuer**
- CARE1* I notice other people's needs before my own.
- CARE2* I feel guilty or selfish when I put myself first.
- CARE3* When someone is suffering, I feel it is up to me to fix it.
- CARE4 People bring me their problems, and I rarely bring them mine.

**HYPV. Hypervigilant / Anxious**
- HYPV1* When someone does not reply, I start imagining what went wrong.
- HYPV2* I scan people and situations for signs that something is off.
- HYPV3* My mind goes to the worst-case scenario.
- HYPV4 I check things more than once or look for reassurance.

**DIST. Distant / Self-sufficient**
- DIST1* When a relationship gets very close, I feel an urge to pull back.
- DIST2* When I need something, I keep it to myself and act as if I am fine.
- DIST3* Receiving care or help makes me uncomfortable.
- DIST4 I tell myself I do not really need anyone.


### Block C. Firefighters

**NUMB. Soothing / Consuming**
- NUMB1* After a hard moment I reach for screens, food, a drink or something else to switch off.
- NUMB2* I soothe difficult feelings with something quick, even when I know it will cost me later.
- NUMB3* Once I start (scrolling, eating, drinking, working), I find it hard to stop.
- NUMB4 I keep myself busy so that I do not have to feel.

**DISS. Checking out / Dissociating** (proposed)
- DISS1* When painful feelings come up, I go flat or blank.
- DISS2* In tense situations I feel far away, as if I were watching from outside.
- DISS3* My mind goes foggy when a conversation touches something painful.
- DISS4 I realize I have been on autopilot for hours.

**ANGR. Protective anger / Explosive**
- ANGR1* When I feel dismissed or humiliated, I react fast and with force.
- ANGR2* My anger comes before I have had time to think.
- ANGR3* I use sarcasm or a raised voice to end a conversation.
- ANGR4 When someone crosses my boundaries, I go on the attack.

**IMPL. Impulsive / Escaping** (proposed)
- IMPL1* When pressure builds, I make sudden decisions just to get out of it (quitting, leaving, ending things).
- IMPL2* When I feel bad, I spend money or take risks on impulse.
- IMPL3* When I am upset, I act on impulse in ways that later make no sense to me.
- IMPL4 When I feel trapped, I get the urge to drop everything and run.


### Block D. Mixed role

**REBL. Rebel / Defiant**
- REBL1* When I am told what to do, I feel an immediate "no" inside.
- REBL2* I resist even good suggestions if they feel like pressure.
- REBL3* Rules that are imposed on me make me want to break them.
- REBL4 When something is demanded of me, I quietly do not do it.


### Block E. Exiles (burden themes)

**SHAM. Not enough / Shame** (proposed)
- SHAM1* A small failure can make me feel worthless.
- SHAM2* There are moments when I feel that something is fundamentally wrong with me.
- SHAM3* Deep down I doubt that I am enough.
- SHAM4 I feel ashamed of who I am, not only of what I did.

**ABAN. Abandoned / Unlovable** (proposed)
- ABAN1* When someone I care about becomes distant, I feel an ache that seems bigger than the situation.
- ABAN2* I fear that people will leave once they really know me.
- ABAN3* Deep down I doubt that I can be loved as I am.
- ABAN4 I feel like the one who is left out or not chosen.

**FEAR. Unsafe / Frightened** (proposed)
- FEAR1* I get waves of fear without a clear reason in the present.
- FEAR2* I suddenly feel small and frightened, like a child.
- FEAR3* My body reacts as if I were in danger when nothing is actually happening.
- FEAR4 A part of me feels that the world is not a safe place.

**POWL. Powerless / Unseen** (proposed)
- POWL1* I feel that what I want or say makes no difference.
- POWL2* In some situations I freeze and cannot stand up for myself.
- POWL3* I feel invisible, as if my needs did not count.
- POWL4 I feel trapped, with no way to change things.

**LONE. Lonely / Grieving** (proposed)
- LONE1* I feel a deep loneliness even when I am with people.
- LONE2* A sadness comes over me that feels much older than anything happening now.
- LONE3* I long for a kind of care or closeness that I never got.
- LONE4 I feel an emptiness inside that nothing quite fills.


## 5. Scoring rules

All thresholds live in one configuration object and are heuristic. They are listed here so trainers can explain a result; they must never be presented as norms.


### 5.1 Scale scores and bands

Scale score = mean of its items, 1.0 to 5.0. Display value 0 to 100 = (mean − 1) / 4 × 100.

| Band | Part scales | Self |
| --- | --- | --- |
| below 2.5 | quiet | hard to reach right now |
| 2.5 to 3.49 | present | available at times |
| 3.5 and above | very active | often available |


### 5.2 Group lead scores

Groups have different numbers of scales (9, 4, 5), so a plain mean would dilute managers. Each lead score is the mean of the two highest scales in the group: managerLead, firefighterLead, exileLead. REBL is excluded from lead scores (its role is mixed) but included in the protector ranking. protectionLoad = mean of all 14 protector scales, shown to facilitators only.


### 5.3 Who is leading: the system pattern

Evaluated in this order; the first match wins.

| # | Pattern | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | SELF_LED | SELF ≥ 3.5 and all three lead scores < 3 |
| 2 | FLOODED (exiles leading) | exileLead ≥ 3.5 and exileLead ≥ each protector lead − 0.3 |
| 3 | REACTIVE (firefighters leading) | firefighterLead ≥ 3 and ≥ managerLead + 0.3 |
| 4 | MANAGED (managers leading) | managerLead ≥ 3 and ≥ firefighterLead + 0.3 |
| 5 | POLARISED | both ≥ 3 and within 0.3 of each other |
| 6 | QUIET_OR_GUARDED | none of the above (SELF < 3.5) |

Modifiers: HIDDEN_EXILES is attached when the pattern is MANAGED, REACTIVE or POLARISED and exileLead < 2.5. SELF_PRESENT is attached when the pattern is not SELF_LED and SELF ≥ 3.5. A gap of exactly 0.3 between managers and firefighters resolves to MANAGED or REACTIVE, not POLARISED, because of the evaluation order.


### 5.4 Leading parts

Protectors (14 scales) and exiles (5 scales) are ranked separately and never against each other. The top protector is called “leading” only if it is at least 3 and at least 0.4 above the second; otherwise the top two are presented as “a team of protectors”, plus the third when it lies within 0.4 of the second. Ties break by the count of items answered 4 or 5, then alphabetically by key.

### 5.6 Response-quality flags and care flag

Stored and shown to facilitators only. STRAIGHT_LINING: the same answer on 90% or more of items. TOO_FAST: completion under 4 minutes on the full form or 3 on the short form. ACQUIESCENCE: SELF ≥ 4 and protectionLoad ≥ 4, which is theoretically unlikely.

Care flag (cohort mode, facilitators only): pattern FLOODED, or exileLead ≥ 4. Labelled “may benefit from extra support”, never as risk or pathology. It is information for pacing the retreat work, not a screening result.


## 6. The results experience

Order of the results page, fixed by design and enforced by a test: framing paragraph; “Who is leading?” with a diagram (Self at the centre, exile feelings around it, firefighters, then managers as the outer ring) and the pattern text; Self panel; protector profile (14 bars grouped Managers, Firefighters, Mixed); detail cards for the top three protectors; “What is being protected” (exiles, always after protectors); the “Meet this part” reflection for the leading protector only; care note; actions (PDF, email, retake); Level II panel in cohort mode; program invitation in public mode. When the pattern is FLOODED the care note moves to the top, support resources are shown, and the invitation is hidden.

Fixed copy: “This is a map of how your inner system is organised right now, not a label. Parts are not you. The real knowing comes from getting to know them.” · “In IFS the Self is never damaged or missing. It is only more or less crowded out by parts that have had to work hard.” · “If this brought up strong feelings, slow down and reach out to someone you trust or to a mental health professional. This tool is not a substitute for professional support.” · “This is a self-report reflection tool. It is not a validated psychometric instrument, and it does not assess or identify any condition. Bands and thresholds are the school's heuristics, not norms.”

Colours carry no good/bad meaning and red is never used. Score colours: Self gold, Managers navy, Firefighters copper, Exiles slate. Wound language is always tentative (“Parts like this often protect…”, “You may recognise…”); the app never asserts a biographical fact about the person. The micro-question printed on every protector card: “What are you keeping me from feeling or remembering when you do this?”.


### 6.1 Pattern texts

**SELF_LED: Self is leading.** Right now your answers suggest that Self-energy is available most of the time and that no group of parts is running the system. Parts are still there, and some are active, but they seem to trust you enough to step back. This is a good moment to get to know them while things are calm.

**FLOODED: Exile feelings are breaking through.** Your answers suggest that old, tender feelings are surfacing often at the moment, more than the protectors can hold back. This is not a failure of any part. It usually means something is asking to be heard. Please go slowly, and let this be met in a held space rather than alone.

**REACTIVE: Firefighters are leading.** Your answers suggest that fast-acting protectors are taking the lead: parts that act quickly to switch off pain once it breaks through. They are working hard and their methods can be costly. They are not the problem; they are the response to something underneath.

**MANAGED: Managers are leading.** Your answers suggest that proactive protectors are organising daily life: parts that plan, check, please, control or keep distance so that tender places are never touched. They have probably been very effective. The cost tends to be effort, rigidity and a sense of not quite living.

**POLARISED: Managers and firefighters both strong.** Your answers suggest two groups of protectors working hard at the same time: some holding things together, others breaking free when the pressure becomes too much. Systems like this often feel like a tug-of-war. Both sides are trying to help, and both deserve curiosity.

**QUIET_OR_GUARDED: Quiet, or guarded.** Your answers show low activity across the system, with Self-energy not clearly available. This can mean a calm period. It can also mean that a part answered on your behalf, keeping things at a distance. Either is fine. You might get curious about which it is.

**HIDDEN_EXILES note.** Low exile scores alongside strong protectors usually mean the protection is working, not that nothing is being protected.

**SELF_PRESENT note.** Self-energy is available alongside the active parts. That is the best condition for getting to know them.

## 8. The five exile themes

These are young parts carrying old burdens. The burden is not who they are. In IFS we do not go to them directly: we first earn the trust of the protectors who stand guard over them. This is work for a held space, with a facilitator or therapist, not something to push into alone.

When protectors are strong and exile scores low, the page shows instead: “Your answers show strong protectors and little exile feeling breaking through. In IFS this usually means the protection is working, not that nothing is being protected. There is nothing to chase here. Getting to know the protectors is the way in, when and if you choose.”

| Key | Name | How it can feel when it breaks through | Burden it may carry | What it longs for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SHAM | Not enough / Shame | Collapse after small failures, wanting to hide, a sense of being defective. | "Something is wrong with me." | To be seen and accepted as it is. |
| ABAN | Abandoned / Unlovable | Ache or panic when someone you care about withdraws, feeling not chosen. | "I will be left. I cannot be loved as I am." | To be stayed with. |
| FEAR | Unsafe / Frightened | Waves of fear, a small and frightened feeling, a body on alert. | "I am not safe." | Protection and a calm presence nearby. |
| POWL | Powerless / Unseen | Freezing, feeling invisible or trapped, no voice. | "What I want does not matter." | To have a voice and to be defended. |
| LONE | Lonely / Grieving | Old sadness, emptiness, longing for something never received. | "I am alone with this." | Company, and room to grieve. |


## 9. Cohort mode and the facilitator dashboard

Participants join with a cohort access code and sign in by an emailed six-digit code. They consent explicitly, with the date and policy version recorded, to storage under their account and to visibility by the named facilitators of their cohort; a separate, unticked box covers the newsletter. They can retake (each attempt kept with its timestamp), view any earlier map, keep a private note on the leading protector that they may share with the facilitator, export everything as JSON and delete their account, which is a real deletion with cascade.

Facilitators see only cohorts they are assigned to, enforced in the database. The cohort page lists participants with completion status, pattern, Self score, top three protectors, top exile theme, quality flags and the care flag; a cohort picture (pattern distribution, leading protectors, mean profile) appears once at least 5 participants have completed; item-level data can be downloaded as a pseudonymised CSV for psychometric analysis. Admins create cohorts (the access code is shown once and stored only as a hash), assign facilitators by email, download an identified CSV and read the audit log, which records every profile view and export.


## 10. Data protection

All response data is treated as special category data. Public results are stored only with explicit consent, kept 6 months, and deletable at any time through a one-click link in the results email. Cohort data is kept 12 months after the cohort end date unless set otherwise, then deleted by a nightly job. No date of birth, no IP addresses in the database, no analytics cookies. Logs record job outcomes, never answers or email addresses. Everything is stored and processed in the European Union: Vercel (hosting, Frankfurt), Neon (database and sign-in, Frankfurt), Resend (email, EU), Upstash (rate limiting, EU). Minimum age: 18, self-declared. The privacy notice in the app is a placeholder pending legal review.


## 11. Languages

English is the master. Spanish (neutral, “tú”), Romanian and Turkish (“sen”) are drafts pending native-speaker review; the Turkish item bank received one review round. Specialised terms stay untranslated in every language: Yesod, Tiferet, Kay Pacha, Self (capitalised, the IFS Self). IFS role names appear with the English in parentheses on first use, for example “Bombero (Firefighter)”. Words never used in user-facing copy, in any language: diagnosis, disorder, clinical, scientifically validated, and their equivalents.


## 12. Glossary

| Term | Meaning in this test |
| --- | --- |
| Self | The undamaged core of the person and the natural leader of the system. Measured by the SELF scale from the eight C's (calm, curiosity, compassion, clarity, confidence, courage, creativity, connectedness). |
| Part | A subpersonality with its own feelings, beliefs and role. Never the whole person. |
| Manager | A proactive protector that organises life so tender places are never touched. |
| Firefighter | A reactive protector that acts fast to put out pain once it breaks through. |
| Exile | A young part carrying a burden, kept out of awareness by protectors. |
| Burden | An extreme belief or feeling a part took on from experience; not the part's nature. |
| Blending | When a part takes over and speaks or acts as if it were the whole person. |
| Self-leadership | The Self leading the system with parts trusting it enough to step back. |
| Triangle of Awareness | The school's frame for a protector: Strategy, Wound, Cost, plus Triggers and Protective Need. |
| System pattern | The test's summary of who is leading: SELF_LED, FLOODED, REACTIVE, MANAGED, POLARISED or QUIET_OR_GUARDED. |
| Care flag | A facilitator-only marker meaning “may benefit from extra support”; pacing information, not a risk score. |
| Short form / full form | 63 items (public default) or 84 items (cohort default) from the same bank. |


## 13. Guidance for people writing about the test

- Use part language throughout. Say “a part of you that plans and controls”, never “you are a controller”.
- Present every part, including firefighters, as having formed for a reason and as protecting something. Never rank parts as better or worse.
- Explain the exile limit honestly: low exile scores with strong protectors usually mean the protection is working.
- Keep the order: protectors first, exiles after, and any exercise addressed to a protector only.
- Call the thresholds heuristics. Do not call the test validated, clinical or diagnostic, and do not compare a person to a population.
- Do not promise transformation. The school's voice hedges the promise: “a framework for…”, “the conditions for…”.
- In cohort contexts, remember the care flag is for pacing, and facilitators only see participants who have consented.
- When the S.W.C.I.R. exercise text is finalised, it belongs in the content files, not in slides alone, so the app and the training stay aligned.
